About News Research

Junze Ye (Tony)

I work on efficient post-training and alignment of AI models under data or compute constraints. My recent work includes on-policy teacher data collection for LLM agents, adaptive physician querying for clinical benchmark labeling, and inference-time fusion of generative experts.

I am a 4-th year PhD candidate at Stanford University. I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Mohsen Bayati and supported by the Program in Operations, Information, and Technology. Previously, I completed my B.S.E. at Princeton University, where I worked with Prof. Jason Klusowski on statistical machine learning and model interpretability.

Selected research

  1. A Few Teacher Steps Go a Long Way: Cost-Efficient On-Policy Data Augmentation for Agent Post-Training

    Junze Ye*, Jiayi Cheng*, Miao Lu*, Michal Mankowski, Jose Blanchet, Mohsen Bayati

    NeurIPS 2026 · Presented at ICML 2026 RLxF Workshop

    Allocating a limited teacher budget to short continuations across more student-reached contexts makes agent post-training more cost-efficient.

    arXiv NeurIPS 2026 ICML 2026 RLxF Workshop

  2. Value-Filtered Expert Query for Efficient Language Agent Finetuning: an Empirical Study

    Junze Ye, Miao Lu, Jose Blanchet, Mohsen Bayati

    NeurIPS 2026 MLxOR Workshop

    Prioritizing teacher queries with a learned value function improves language-agent finetuning when expert-labeling budgets are small.

    Paper Workshop

  3. Scalable Stewardship of an LLM-Assisted Clinical Benchmark with Physician Oversight

    Junze Ye, Daniel Tawfik, Alex J. Goodell, Nikhil V. Kotha, Mark K. Buyyounouski, Mohsen Bayati

    Preprint, under journal review · Finalist, 2026 INFORMS HAS Best Student Paper Competition

    A physician-in-the-loop approach to finding and correcting benchmark label errors, and studying their effects on model evaluation and training.

    arXiv Code & data

  4. ScoreFusion: Fusing Score-based Generative Models via Kullback–Leibler Barycenters

    Hao Liu*, Junze Ye*, Jose Blanchet, Nian Si

    AISTATS 2025 · Oral (top 2% accepted)

    Combining pretrained denoising diffusion models to learn a target distribution from limited data, with a theoretical foundation in KL-divergence barycenters.
